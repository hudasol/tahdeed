## What is this about?

We are building an **open benchmark for UAE address resolution**: software that turns a description such as "near the main gate, opposite the petrol station, Al Wahda" into a place on the map. Real wording from real residents is the only honest way to test such software, so we are asking volunteers to describe, in their own words, how they would tell a taxi or delivery driver to get to five **public places** (malls, hospitals, metro stations, universities, public buildings, parks).

## What will you do?

You will see five place names (in English and Arabic) and a category. For each one you type a short description, in Arabic, English, Arabizi (Arabic written with Latin letters and digits, for example "3ala yameen"), or a mix. It takes about 10 minutes. You can skip any place you do not know. You do not need to look anything up.

## What do we collect?

- The **free text** you write for each place, and which place it was for.
- The **language or script** you used (we detect it automatically; you may also tell us).
- **Optional, coarse groups only:** the emirate where you live, and how long you have lived in the UAE (for example "6 to 10 years").

## What do we NOT collect?

Your **name, email address, phone number, IP address**, age, nationality, or any account. The web form works offline, uses no cookies and no analytics, and sends nothing over the internet. It only saves a small file on your own device, which you then send to the organiser yourself.

The organiser gives you a **volunteer code**. The code is used only to keep your five answers together and is replaced by a one-way scrambled value before any analysis. The organiser should not keep a list that links codes to names (or must destroy it once the data collection closes).

## Please do not write personal information

Write about **public places only**. Do **not** write a home address, a villa or flat number, a personal phone number, an email address, a social media name, or anything that could identify a person. The form warns you when a text looks like one of these, and the organiser also screens the texts. Anything that looks like a personal address is removed before publication.

## Taking part is voluntary

You do not have to take part and you may stop at any time without giving a reason. **You can withdraw until you send your file to the organiser** simply by not sending it. After you have sent it, the file carries only your code, so the organiser can delete your answers only if you tell them your code, and only until the dataset is published (planned date: `<PUBLICATION_DATE>`). After publication the data cannot be taken back from copies others have made.

## What will happen to the data?

After personal details are removed, the descriptions will be **published as an anonymised dataset under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence (CC BY-SA 4.0)**, together with the list of places and the results of testing address-resolution software. Anyone may then use and share it, provided they credit the project and share their versions under the same licence. *(This licence is the template's default choice. The owner may choose another one, and must then edit this paragraph, the Arabic version and the form.)*

The raw files you send are stored in an access-controlled, encrypted location, are used only to build the dataset, and are deleted after the dataset has been checked and the withdrawal period has ended (`<DELETE_RAW_BY>`).

## Risks and benefits

We expect no risk beyond the small chance that a text accidentally contains something personal, which is why we screen for it. There is no payment. The benefit is a public test set that makes UAE address software more reliable for everyone.
